# Medieval Minecraft building: techniques and building types (research, 2026-10-10)

Research behind the quality check's rubric (`RUBRIC.md`). Sources at the bottom. The pages themselves could not be
opened from the cloud session (blocked), so this is built from search results of build guides and blogs; they agree
strongly on the main points (depth, overhang, palette, roof, asymmetry). Lines marked *common practice* are standard
builder advice without a source found here.

Our builds are **miniature** (soldiers are half a block tall; a cottage is about 5 x 7 with 3-high walls). Each
technique says whether it works at that size: **OK**, **ADAPT** (smaller version only) or **NO**.

## Techniques

| Technique | What it is | How to see it in a render | Measurable from blocks? | Small scale |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Timber frame | Logs at corners, the floor line and every 2 to 4 blocks; plank or white infill set 1 block in | Dark vertical lines, light panels, shadow lines | Yes: logs at corners, infill one deeper | OK (diagonal bracing: NO, looks boxy) |
| Jetty | The upper floor sticks out 1 block over the floor below, on upside-down stair brackets | Upper floor wider, shadow band under it | Yes: footprint grows with height | ADAPT (two storeys only) |
| Stone base | Cobblestone or stone brick bottom course, timber above | Grey band at the bottom | Yes | OK (one course on 3-high walls) |
| Steep roof with overhang | 45 degrees or steeper, eaves 1 block past the walls, gables overhang too, slab ridge | Shadow under the eaves; roof as tall as the walls or more | Yes: roof outside the wall footprint | OK: the single most important trait |
| Flared eaves | The lowest roof row flatter, pushed out | Roof line bends near the foot | Partly | ADAPT/NO: one slab row at most |
| Mixed roof | 2 to 3 similar roof blocks mixed or in a gradient | Speckled roof, not one flat colour | Yes | OK, subtle (2 blocks) |
| Chimney | Stone or brick column above the roof, often on a gable end, campfire on top for smoke | Vertical stone above the roofline | Yes | OK (1 x 1) |
| Depth layering | Frame 1 block out; stairs, slabs, trapdoors, buttons, fences instead of full blocks; sills; stairs framing openings | Shadows break up every face | Yes: share of non-cube blocks, depths per wall | OK: the main tool at small scale |
| Palette and gradient | One main block plus 2 to 3 accents; darker at the bottom, lighter at the top | Smooth change, no clashing colours | Yes, with a colour table | OK, 2 to 3 blocks per surface |
| Windows | Set in 1 block, with trapdoor shutters or slab sills; rule of thirds; odd widths so a door can be centred | Dark openings with a frame | Yes | ADAPT: 1 x 1 or 1 x 2 panes |
| Flower boxes, vines, foliage | Trapdoors or pots under windows, leaves and vines at corners and the base, bushes, paths | Green near the walls, soft ground line | Yes | OK, cheap and effective |
| Lanterns | On posts or chains, by the door, at different heights | Points of light | Yes | OK |
| Asymmetry | Door off-centre, a side extension, roof parts at different heights, a second smaller roof | Not mirror-symmetric | Yes | ADAPT: a lean-to or porch is enough |
| Posts breaking up walls | No run of the same block at one depth longer than 4 to 5 | Rhythm along the wall | Yes | OK |
| Corbels | Upside-down stairs under jetties, balconies, machicolations | | Yes | OK |
| Buttresses | Thicker wall parts every 8 to 10 blocks | | Yes | ADAPT: every 4 to 5, or posts |
| Proportion | Roof about as tall as the walls or taller; no huge flat boxes; odd widths | | Yes: bounding box ratios | OK: 5 x 7 cottage with a 3 to 4 high roof |

## Building types

- **Cottage:** stone base, timber frame, steep overhanging roof, chimney, one door, 1 to 2 shuttered windows, a flower
  box, a small fence or garden; a loft under the roof.
- **Timber townhouse:** 2 to 3 storeys, jettied floors, narrow gable to the street, dormers.
- **Blacksmith:** open or half-open side under a lean-to roof; stone forge with a chimney (campfire, furnace, blast
  furnace), anvil, grindstone, barrels, hanging chains, lanterns at different heights.
- **Tavern or inn:** the biggest house in the village; two storeys, a sign hanging from a bracket, barrels, benches,
  a big chimney, a stable or yard next to it (*common practice*).
- **Mill:** watermill with a wheel on a river bank; windmill as a tall tapering tower with sails.
- **Stables:** long and low, open front with stalls, fences, hay bales, a deep roof overhang (gates are decoration
  only: soldiers can't open them).
- **Keep or castle:** stone brick and dark oak, thick walls, one courtyard. It must clearly be the tallest mass, or it
  reads as a house.
- **Towers:** 2 to 3 blocks wider than the wall and taller; at corners, at intervals, by the gate; crenellations; a
  cone or pyramid roof is optional.
- **Walls:** crenellations (1 block on, 1 off), a walkway, buttresses or pilasters, machicolations from slabs and stairs.
- **Gatehouse:** an arched opening between two towers, an iron-bar portcullis.
- **Market:** stalls with coloured wool or banner awnings, barrels, crates, an open square, a well or fountain.
- **Church:** stone, one main block plus one accent, a tall tower, tall narrow windows, steep roof, buttresses.
- **Farm:** fenced plots, crops in rows with water channels, a shed or barn, a scarecrow, a composter (*common
  practice*). Carlton's own idea: one big detailed farmhouse, with the fields growing around it in modules.
- **Dock:** stone foundation, wooden piers on log posts in the water, barrels and crates, a crane with chains,
  lanterns.

## Common beginner mistakes

- One block over a big surface: flat and boring.
- Flat walls with no depth.
- A flat roof or no overhang: the biggest giveaway of a beginner.
- A single boxy mass with one big roof.
- Too many materials, or detail everywhere (trapdoor and button spam): leave some calm surfaces.
- Bad proportions: huge flat boxes, tiny windows, a castle no taller than the houses.
- No plants or landscaping: a building on bare grass.

## What needs a picture (not measurable from blocks)

Whether a colour gradient looks good, whether there is too much detail, and whether the building reads as what it is.
That is why the quality check has a visual half.
